"I want a different job, but don't know which" +
What it looks likeA career question. Which role, which sector, which course.
What it often isNot "which job" but "I don't feel seen / free / useful". The job is the packaging; the feeling is the content.
The reframeDon't search for a job title, search for a feeling: when was work last light? What were you doing? Look for jobs that give that feeling, not ones that look good on LinkedIn.
First stepThis week, talk to one person doing work you secretly admire. No application — just curiosity.
"I feel lonely, but don't know what I'm missing" +
What it looks likeToo few people around you.
What it often isNot too few people, but too little contact where you can be yourself. You can be lonely in a full room and not at all at home with one good friend.
The reframeThe question isn't "how do I meet more people", but "around whom do I already feel a bit like myself — and how do I give that more attention?"
First stepSend one message today to someone where it feels light. "I was thinking of you." Nothing more.
"I want more money, but feel guilty about it" +
What it looks likeGreed, or "not spiritual enough".
What it often isA perfectly ordinary, healthy wish to breathe freely, give, and not lie awake. The idea that you must switch off your ego to be allowed to want anything comes from one particular reading of spirituality — and it's not the only one.
The reframeMoney is energy — like breath or water. It may flow. Wanting it doesn't make you a bad person; it makes you someone who wants to take good care of yourself and others.
First stepWrite down what you'd actually do with more money. Ten to one it's about freedom and care, not greed.
"I keep falling for the wrong ones" +
What it looks likeBad luck in love.
What it often isA pattern that pulls in what feels familiar — not what's healthy. We often pick what's familiar, even when it hurts.
The reframeThe question isn't "where do I find a good one", but "what feeling do I seek with someone — and am I giving that to myself already?" What you don't believe about your own worth, you'll see confirmed outside yourself.
First stepMake a short list: how do I want to feel around someone? Use it as compass, not the looks or the chemistry.
"I just don't know what I want with my life" +
What it looks likeA big, paralysing gap. Everyone seems to have a plan except you.
What it often isThe pressure to "know it". Nobody knows the whole picture — the people who look confident make it up as they go too.
The reframeYou don't have to know your whole life. Just the next interesting step. Direction emerges from moving, not from sitting still and waiting for inspiration.
First stepDo one thing this week that makes you curious. Not useful — curious. Follow that thread.
"I want so much I get paralysed and do nothing" +
What it looks likeLaziness or lack of discipline.
What it often isOverwhelm. Seeing ten doors open at once isn't a luxury — it's paralysing. Your brain locks up under too many choices.
The reframeYou don't have to pick what's best for the rest of your life. Pick what attracts you most now, and give it two weeks. You may always switch.
First stepCross out half your list today. Not because it's not allowed — to make air. The rest won't run away.

Getting stuck is part of every journey. Yours too.

Tap what you recognise. No sermon — just a gentle nudge.

"I don't even know what I want" +

No one knows that in one go. Don't start with "what do I want" but "what do I not want anymore". You usually know that flawlessly. Walk to Discover ✦ and do the longing radar — it points the way.

"I want too much at once" +

Longing isn't the problem; fragmentation is. Pick one. Not forever — for now. The other wishes don't evaporate if you take one seriously; they wait their turn.

"I don't believe I deserve it" +

This is the silent interrupter for almost everyone. You don't have to deserve anything to be allowed to want it — a child doesn't have to earn love either. Start small: allow yourself one small thing this week without justifying it. Deserving is a habit of thought, not a fact of the universe.

"It's taking too long, I'm getting impatient" +

Something that's growing, you don't see growing — that's why it feels like nothing is happening. Something is happening. Life has no track & trace, and the stars keep their own clock. Focus on the step, not the arrival.

"I compare myself to everyone" +

Someone else getting something doesn't take anything from you — happiness isn't a cake whose slices run out. Be glad for them, send them a message even, and think: "if she can, I can too." That's the right frequency to step into yourself.

"I start things but never finish" +

Maybe you start things you don't actually want to finish — that's information, not failure. But if it does matter: make your goal ridiculously small. Not "I'll exercise" but "I'll put on my shoes". Done is a feeling you train with tiny wins.
